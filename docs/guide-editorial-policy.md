# Guide editorial policy

Owner decision, 15 November 2026. It applies to every Life in UK Guide, including Family Visa, Work Visa and Settlement.

## What Life in UK is

Life in UK is a multilingual information and guided-navigation website, not an immigration law firm. Its job is to:

- find and organise official UK information;
- translate it into accessible languages;
- explain ordinary processes in plain language;
- guide readers step by step through relevant information and checklists;
- say clearly when a situation cannot reliably be explained from official general guidance;
- direct readers with complex personal circumstances to appropriately regulated advisers. For immigration, these are advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority (IAA), or solicitors.

It does not give individualised immigration advice, eligibility determinations or legal representation, predict outcomes, or claim professional legal review.

## Content rules

1. Publish only factual statements supported by reliable sources. GOV.UK, the Immigration Rules and published Home Office guidance come first.
2. If a claim is uncertain, qualify it accurately or leave it out.
3. Do not speculate, extend case law by analogy, or give individualised legal conclusions.
4. Record the official sources and the date each was verified. Evidence links and the evidence panel carry this for every material statement.
5. Never claim professional or native-speaker review that has not taken place.

## Editorial process

- The internal process is AI-assisted research, source checking (including verbatim quotation checks against fetched official text) and automated tests. Publication goes through the existing owner workflow: batch, translation import, per-locale review, approval, then publish with a checklist, `family-visa-v1` for immigration Guides.
- Professional (solicitor or adviser) review and native-speaker review are **not** mandatory publication gates. They may be added for an individual Guide, and if so they are recorded truthfully.
- Translations keep the legal meaning of the Simplified Chinese source. Official English quotations stay verbatim. Evidence locators are identical in all locales.
