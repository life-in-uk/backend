## Locale treatment

- **zh-CN and zh-TW** retain sourced Chinese-language and China-document examples. Check Mandarin and Cantonese separately, label jurisdiction and don't imply that a Chinese-language preference identifies where a document was issued.
- **English** leads with the practical UK newcomer question. Use England explicitly in Health metadata and copy. For banking, explain where requirements vary by provider and account.
- **Urdu** uses natural Urdu within the right-to-left layout and retains recognisable UK terms such as GP, NHS 111 and Basic Bank Account. A practical interpreter request may say “مجھے اردو مترجم چاہیے” (“I need an Urdu interpreter”). It expresses a language preference rather than an entitlement or availability guarantee.
- **Hindi** uses Hindi in Devanagari with the UK service labels readers need at the point of use. An interpreter request may say “मुझे हिन्दी दुभाषिया चाहिए” (“I need a Hindi interpreter”). Keep it separate from any question about the origin of documents or qualification for care.
- For booking scripts, show a short English sentence a reader can use and a local-language meaning. Do not infer country or nationality from language. Use a country-specific example only when supported by a current authority for that claim, with jurisdiction, scope, date and any exceptions. Treat unverified nationality-based acceptance claims as review items.
